## Verified facts

### Sitting and mortality
- **Ekelund et al. 2016** (read, abstract). Lancet 388(10051):1302-10. Harmonised meta-analysis,
  16 studies; 13 with sitting time: 1,005,791 people, followed 2 to 18.1 years, 84,609 (8.4%) died.
  Referent: sitting <4 h/day and most active quartile (>35.5 MET-h/week).
  - Least active (<2.5 MET-h/week) and sitting >8 h/day: HR 1.59 (1.52-1.66).
  - Sitting >8 h/day but most active: HR 1.04 (0.99-1.10), no increased risk.
  - Sitting <4 h/day but least active: HR 1.27 (1.22-1.31).
  - About 60-75 min/day of moderate activity "seem to eliminate" the risk with high sitting time;
    attenuates but does not eliminate the risk with high TV time (TV >=5 h/day in most active
    quartile: HR 1.16, 1.05-1.28).
  - Observational: association, not proof of cause.
- **Vallance et al. 2018** (read, abstract). Am J Public Health 108(11):1478-82. "Is sitting really
  the new smoking?" Sedentary behaviour all-cause HR 1.22 (1.09-1.41) vs current smokers RR 2.80
  (men) and 2.76 (women); heavy smokers (>40/day) RR 4.08 men, 4.41 women. Absolute: >2000 excess
  deaths per 100,000 per year for heaviest smokers vs 190 for highest vs lowest sitting.
- "Sitting is the new smoking" as a phrase: usually attributed to James Levine (reported, not read).

### Breaking up sitting
- **Dunstan et al. 2012** (read, abstract). Diabetes Care 35(5):976-83. n=19 overweight/obese adults,
  45-65 years, randomised crossover: uninterrupted sitting vs 2-min light or moderate walking every
  20 min, over 5 h after a test drink. Glucose iAUC 6.9 sitting vs 5.2 light vs 4.9 moderate
  (mmol/L.h); insulin iAUC 828.6 vs 633.6 vs 637.6 (pmol/L.h). About a quarter lower (glucose
  -25% light, -29% moderate; insulin -24% and -23%, computed from the abstract means). Acute, lab,
  small, overweight adults: do not generalise to "breaks prevent diabetes".
- **Shrestha et al. 2018 Cochrane** (read, abstract). CD010912.pub5. 34 studies, 3,397 people,
  high-income countries. Sit-stand desks: -100 min/workday sitting at short term (up to 3 months;
  95% CI -116 to -84; 10 studies; low-quality evidence); -57 min/day at 3-12 months (2 studies).
  Short breaks of 1-2 min every half hour: -40 min/day vs two 15-min breaks (1 study, low quality).
  Computer prompts + information: -14 min/day short term (not significant), -55 min/day medium
  term (1 study); prompting cut the number (-1.1) and duration (-74 min/day) of sitting bouts of
  30 min or more (1 study). No studies of standing or walking meetings. No evidence beyond 12 months.
- **Albulescu et al. 2022** (read, abstract). PLoS One 17(8):e0272460. Micro-breaks (10 min or less),
  22 samples, N=2,335. Vigor d=.36, fatigue d=.35 (small, significant); overall performance d=.16,
  not significant; performance helped only for less cognitively demanding tasks; longer breaks,
  bigger performance boost.

### Exercise snacks
- **Jenkins et al. 2019** (read, abstract). Appl Physiol Nutr Metab 44(6):681-4. Sedentary young
  adults, n=12 per group: 3 bouts/day of vigorously climbing 3 flights (60 steps), 1-4 h apart,
  3 days/week, 6 weeks. Peak oxygen uptake higher than controls (P=0.003), "the absolute increase
  was modest". Tiny trial.
- **Stamatakis et al. 2022** (read, abstract). Nat Med 28(12):2521-9. UK Biobank, 25,241
  non-exercisers, mean age 61.8, 6.9 years follow-up, 852 deaths. Median 3 VILPA bouts/day (1-2 min)
  associated with 38-40% lower all-cause and cancer mortality, 48-49% lower CVD mortality.
  Observational, wrist accelerometer, association only: teach as "promising, not proven".

### Steps
- **Paluch et al. 2022** (read, abstract). Lancet Public Health 7(3):e219-28. 15 cohorts, 47,471
  adults, 3,013 deaths. Risk falls with steps until about 6,000-8,000/day (60+) and 8,000-10,000
  (under 60). Abstract: 10,000 is widely promoted with "little evidence".
- **Lee et al. 2019** (read, abstract). JAMA Intern Med 179(8):1105-12. 16,741 older women, mean age
  72: about 4,400 steps/day already linked to lower mortality than about 2,700; levels off around
  7,500. Origin of "10,000 steps" in a 1960s Japanese pedometer brand: reported, not read.

### Posture
- **Slater et al. 2019** (read, abstract). "Sit up straight": time to re-evaluate. J Orthop Sports
  Phys Ther 49(8):562-4. Viewpoint: belief that spinal pain is caused by sitting or bending
  "incorrectly" lacks strong evidence; a posture industry flourished anyway.
  CORRECTION to the build brief: the brief said BJSM editorial; PubMed lists it in JOSPT.
- **NHS neck pain page** (read, 2026-10-12) lists "bad posture" at a desk as a common cause and
  advises "sit upright" and not keeping the neck in one position. This disagrees in emphasis with
  Slater et al. Teach the disagreement: both agree that staying in one position for long is the
  problem; they differ on whether there is a "correct" position.

### Eyes
- **Talens-Estarelles et al. 2023** (read, abstract). Cont Lens Anterior Eye 46(2):101744.
  29 symptomatic computer users, 2 weeks of 20-20-20 reminders: digital eye strain and dry eye
  symptoms fell (p<=.045); not maintained one week after stopping; no change in tear film or
  ocular surface signs; binocular vision mostly unchanged. No separate control group (before/after).
- 20-20-20 rule origin: usually credited to optometrists, no single source found (reported).
- **AAO "Computers, Digital Devices, and Eye Strain"** (read, published 2024-06-27): we blink about
  15 times a minute normally, 5 to 7 at screens; sit about 25 inches (arm's length) away; screens
  do not permanently damage eyes. The page advises looking up and away; it does not name 20-20-20.

### Desk setup
- **OSHA computer workstations eTool, monitors** (read): viewing distance 20-40 inches (50-100 cm);
  top of monitor at or slightly below eye level; centre 15-20 degrees below horizontal eye level.

### When to see a professional (NHS, read 2026-10-12)
- Back pain, see a GP: not better after a few weeks at home; stopping daily activities; worse at
  night or after rest; weight loss without trying; from between the shoulders. Urgent (111): feel
  hot, shivery or unwell; severe pain that starts suddenly or worsens quickly. 999: pain, tingling,
  weakness or numbness in both legs; numbness around genitals or bottom; bladder or bowel changes;
  chest pain; after a serious accident. Advice: stay active, do not stay in bed long.
- Neck pain, see a GP: not gone after a few weeks; painkillers not working; pins and needles or a
  cold arm.
- Carpal tunnel: ache, numbness, tingling, weak thumb, usually worse at night; see a GP if getting
  worse or not going away.

### Habit
- **Lally et al. 2010** (reported; Eur J Soc Psychol 40(6):998-1009, abstract not reachable, 403).
  96 volunteers, one daily behaviour for 12 weeks; median about 66 days to automaticity, modelled
  range 18 to 254 days; missing one day did not derail it. Same numbers already live in
  learn-meditation session 6. Never quote 66 without the range.
- Cue-based prompts: Cochrane above (computer prompts) is the only workplace evidence read here.

### Claims tiered (wellbeing myths list)
- "Sitting is the new smoking": overstated; Vallance 2018 numbers (read).
- "10,000 steps": no special evidence for that number; Paluch 2022 and Lee 2019 (read).
- "Perfect posture prevents pain": contested; Slater 2019 vs NHS neck page (both read).
- "Attention span shorter than a goldfish", "8 glasses of water", "power poses", "ego depletion",
  "cortisol belly", "physiological sigh resets stress": NOT taught; out of scope (stress and
  breathing belong to learn-stress-control).

## Per-session coverage

1. **What nine hours of sitting really does** - Ekelund 2016, Vallance 2018 ("new smoking"
   tiered), Dunstan 2012 mechanism (muscle inactive, glucose). Not medical advice. Build-along:
   log your own sitting bouts for a morning.
2. **There is no perfect posture** - Slater 2019 vs NHS neck page; the next posture is the best
   posture; OSHA monitor basics; desk setup checklist. Build-along: 10-minute desk setup.
3. **The three-minute routine** - six moves (neck, shoulders, reach, hip, wrist, march + look far),
   reps or seconds, safe range, stop-if. Albulescu 2022 (small effects). Seams to taichi/yoga.
4. **The break bench** - the widget, canon above, Cochrane short breaks and prompts, routine timer.
5. **Eyes, wrists and the screen** - AAO blinking and distance, Talens-Estarelles 2023 (20-20-20
   tiered), carpal tunnel signs (NHS), wrist moves, OSHA distance/height.
6. **Making the break automatic** - cues and calendar nudges (Cochrane prompts), Lally (reported,
   range), exercise snacks (Jenkins 2019, Stamatakis 2022 tiered), steps (Paluch, Lee; 10,000
   tiered), when pain needs a professional (NHS lists). Build-along: the break plan.

## Not covered (honest list)
- Diagnosis or treatment of any condition; pregnancy, post-surgery, or specific injuries.
- Breathing techniques and stress physiology (learn-stress-control).
- Full exercise programming, strength training plans, sports.
- Long-term (>12 months) effects of any workplace intervention: the Cochrane review found none.
- Treadmill and cycling desks beyond "unclear or inconsistent" (Cochrane).
- Lighting, noise, temperature and nutrition at the desk.
