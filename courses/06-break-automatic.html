Part 1 · covers cues and workplace prompts

Cue the break, do not will it

5 min live

A clock cue fires at a time, whatever you are doing. An event cue fires when something happens: a call ends, you refill your water, you send a report. A place cue fires where you are: the printer, the kitchen, the top of the stairs. Clock cues are the easiest to set and the easiest to lose, because they ignore your calendar.

LiveWhy 50-minute meetings are not enough on their own3 min▶

A popular fix is to end meetings ten minutes early. Run it on the bench: in "My own day", give Priyanka's afternoon as four seated meetings, 13:00-13:50, 14:00-14:50, 15:00-15:50 and 16:00-16:50, and choose rung 3. The longest sit is still 260 minutes and 12 of 17 cues are still dropped, exactly as with back-to-back hours. The ten-minute gaps exist, but the clock cues fire at :00 and :30, which are inside meetings.

The gap only helps if the break is tied to it. "When a meeting ends, I stand and do the routine before I open email" is an event cue, and it lands in exactly the minutes the shorter meetings freed. The bench does not model event cues, so there is no number for this on the page. It is the next experiment to run on your own day.

Self-studyWhat prompts achieved in workplace trials3 min▶

The 2018 Cochrane review (Shrestha and colleagues) found that computer prompts combined with information made no significant change in sitting at short-term follow-up (about 14 minutes a day less, three studies), but in one study a significant cut of about 55 minutes a day at medium term. In one study, prompting also cut the number of sitting bouts of 30 minutes or more by about one a day. All of it is low-quality evidence from small trials.

Read that honestly: prompts help some people somewhat, and the effect may take time to show. It is a reason to design the cue well, not a promise that a pop-up will change your day.

Real world

The refill rule. A familiar trick among desk workers: use a small water glass instead of a large bottle, so you have to get up to refill it. It is a place cue with a built-in timer that drinking sets, not the clock. It has no trial behind it, and it does not need one to be worth trying; just do not quote it as evidence.

Part 2 · covers habit timelines, exercise snacks and steps

Snacks, steps and how long a habit takes

5 min live

Three popular claims arrive with any break plan: a habit takes 21 days, a few flights of stairs are as good as a workout, and you need 10,000 steps. Each has a real study nearby and a myth on top. The figure lays out how strong each piece of evidence actually is.

LiveExercise snacks, sized honestly3 min▶

Jenkins and colleagues (2019) had sedentary young adults climb three flights, 60 steps, vigorously, three times a day with one to four hours between, three days a week for six weeks; 12 people per group. Peak oxygen uptake rose compared with controls, and the authors themselves call the absolute increase modest. Stamatakis and colleagues (2022) used wrist accelerometers on 25,241 UK Biobank non-exercisers with an average age near 62: about three short vigorous bouts a day, one or two minutes each, were linked to 38 to 40 percent lower all-cause and cancer mortality over about seven years.

The second number is far bigger and far weaker. It is an association; people who naturally hurry up stairs may differ in many ways. Teach it as promising, not proven. For Priyanka the practical message is simple and safe: taking the stairs briskly once or twice a day is a reasonable snack, if her heart and joints are healthy. Anyone with heart symptoms, chest pain on exertion or a joint problem asks a doctor first.

LiveHow long the break habit takes2 min▶

The best-known study of habit formation (Lally and colleagues, 2010; reported, the paper was not read at source) followed 96 volunteers repeating one daily behaviour. The median time for it to feel automatic was about 66 days, and the modelled range ran from 18 to 254. Missing a single day did not derail it. So "21 days" has no basis there, and 66 is a middle, not a deadline. Plan for weeks to months, and treat a missed day as normal.

Self-studyWhere 10,000 steps came from, and what the data say3 min▶

The 10,000 figure is often traced to a 1960s Japanese pedometer brand (reported). Paluch and colleagues' 2022 meta-analysis of 15 cohorts states plainly that 10,000 is widely promoted with little evidence; mortality risk fell with more steps until about 6,000 to 8,000 a day for adults over 60 and 8,000 to 10,000 for younger adults. Lee and colleagues (2019) found, in 16,741 older women, that about 4,400 steps a day was already linked to lower mortality than about 2,700, levelling off around 7,500.

None of this says steps replace breaks. A step target and a break plan answer the two different questions from session 1: how much you move, and how long you sit at a stretch.

Part 3 · covers when pain needs a professional

When to stop stretching and see someone

5 min live

Desk aches are common and usually settle with movement. Some symptoms are not desk aches. The NHS publishes plain lists for back pain, and shorter ones for neck pain and wrist tingling. This is the one part of the six sessions where the right move is to stop following the pages and get examined.

LiveNeck, wrists and chest: the short lists3 min▶

Neck. The NHS advises seeing a GP if neck pain or stiffness does not go away after a few weeks, painkillers have not worked, you are worried, or you have other symptoms such as pins and needles or a cold arm. Wrists and hands. Numbness, tingling, an ache in the fingers or a weak thumb, often worse at night, can be carpal tunnel syndrome; see a GP if it is getting worse or not going away (session 5). Chest pain is never a desk ache: call emergency services.

None of the moves in session 3 is a treatment for any of these. If a move brings on tingling, numbness, pain down an arm or leg, or dizziness, stop it and get it checked.

Self-studyWhy "keep moving" is the default advice2 min▶

For ordinary back pain, the NHS advice is to stay active and continue daily activities, and not to stay in bed for long periods. That matches the thread through all six sessions: the problem is usually staying still, not moving. It is still general guidance for the common case. A physiotherapist who examines you can tell you what applies to your back, which no web page can.

Real world

The colleague who stretched through a numb foot. A familiar pattern: someone with back pain finds a stretch online and keeps doing it while a new symptom, a numb foot or tingling down one leg, gets worse. The stretch was never the issue; the new symptom was the signal to stop and be seen. Movement advice is for ordinary aches. New neurological symptoms are a different category.

Build-along

Priyanka's break plan, then yours

★ 22 min · everyone builds

One page, five lines, built from her bench numbers and her calendar (constructed). Write yours alongside, using your own bench run from session 4. Every line is something that happens without deciding to do it in the moment.

Write down your two bench numbers and your meeting wall. From session 4: your rung 3 longest sit and dropped cues, and the block of the week with the most back-to-back seated meetings.

★ Step 1 · Priyanka's starting point (constructed)Ordinary day, rung 3: longest sit 87 min, 7 of 17 cues dropped Thursday, 13:00-17:00: longest sit 260 min, 12 of 17 cues dropped Meeting wall: Thursday afternoon

Anchor three breaks to events, not times. Pick things that already happen every day. Write each as "when X, I do Y".

★ Step 2 · three event and place cuesWhen a meeting or call ends → stand, 3-minute routine, before opening email When I come back from lunch → stairs up one floor and back When I refill my water glass → look far for 20 seconds, wrist circles

Change one thing on the meeting wall. Only meetings you own or can ask about. Shorter meetings matter only because step 2 tied a break to their end.

★ Step 3 · one calendar changeOwn the 13:00 and 16:00 meetings → cut to 50 minutes 1:1 with manager → ask: "walk it?" Keep the clock reminder only as a backup for desk mornings

Set an honest timeline. Habits took between 18 and 254 days in the best-known study (reported). Decide now what you will do on a missed day, so it does not become a missed week.

★ Step 4 · expectationsReview after 4 weeks, again after 10. Missed a day? Do the next cue. No catch-up, no streak to save.

Write your red-flag lines at the bottom of the page. Copy them from Part 3 so they sit next to the plan, not in your memory.

★ Step 5 · stop and see someone if- numbness, tingling or weakness in both legs, bladder or bowel changes, chest pain → 999 / A&E - severe pain that starts suddenly or worsens fast, or feeling unwell with it → urgent GP / 111 - not better after a few weeks, worse at night, tingling in a hand that persists → GP

Re-run the bench for your mornings. Enter tomorrow's calendar in "My own day" and check the rung 3 longest sit on the days without a meeting wall. Write it next to step 1's number; that is the part of your plan the bench can measure.

Homework

After the last session

10 min a day
  1. Run the plan for one week. Tick each cue that fired. Do not judge the misses; count them.
  2. Make one calendar ask. Shorten one meeting you own or ask to walk one one-to-one.
  3. Log one day again, the same way as session 1, and compare your longest sit with your first log.
  4. Pin the red-flag lines where you will see them, and share them with one colleague who complains about their back.

1 · Priyanka cuts her Thursday meetings to 50 minutes but keeps her half-hourly clock reminder. What does the bench show for rung 3?

The gaps sit at :50 to :00, and no clock cue lands there. Shorter meetings only help when the break is anchored to the end of the meeting, an event cue, which the bench does not model.

2 · What is the honest summary of Stamatakis and colleagues' 2022 finding on short vigorous bursts?

25,241 UK Biobank non-exercisers wore wrist accelerometers; the 38 to 40 percent figure is an association. The 12-per-group study is Jenkins 2019, a small trial with a modest fitness gain.

3 · Which symptom with back pain means calling 999 or going to A&E, according to the NHS?

These are the emergency signs on the NHS back pain page, along with numbness around the genitals or bottom, chest pain, and pain after a serious accident. Ordinary stiffness and soreness are the common case where staying active is the advice.
